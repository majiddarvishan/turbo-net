Zero-copy writes
Instead of concatenating header+body into one big std::vector<uint8_t>, you can issue a scatter/gather write (two buffers: one for the header, one for the body). That skips the extra memory copy and allocation for large payloads.

Buffer pooling / reuse
Allocating and freeing network buffers on every send/receive is costly. A simple object-pool (e.g. a boost::lockfree::stack of pre-allocated std::vector<uint8_t>) lets you recycle buffers.

Avoid std::deque of boost::asio::const_buffer
Instead of a std::deque, you can use a flat ring buffer for pending writes (indexing into a single contiguous array) which reduces pointer indirection and allocations.

Minimize strand overhead
If you don’t share a single Connection across multiple threads, you can drop the strand entirely and rely on one io_context thread per connection.

Reserve containers up front
Call .reserve() on your buffer pools and on your std::unordered_map of pending requests to avoid re-hashing under load.

Batch small messages
If you know you’re sending many small packets, consider coalescing them into one larger asynchronous write.
